From design to repeatable manufacturing

A design becomes a product only when the manufacturing process can reproduce it consistently and at yield. My internships made me interested in the engineering between an intended result and the process conditions that repeatedly produce it.

From design to process

Studying digital hardware taught me how a chip is designed. Manufacturing taught me a different problem: a process has to reproduce that design consistently across tools, lots, and operating conditions. That is the side of engineering I found myself most drawn to.

Why process integration at TSMC

What interests me about process integration is that the answer rarely lives in one place. Process conditions, equipment behavior, inspection data, electrical results, and downstream yield have to be read together. My manufacturing experience taught me to work that way, and I want to develop that skill at semiconductor scale.

What I bring

I look across the process instead of stopping at the step where a problem becomes visible. I start from evidence, test a defined hypothesis or controlled change, and verify whether the result holds downstream before treating the work as complete. My digital hardware background also gives me context for the downstream side of process integration: the circuit and system behavior that device and interconnect variation eventually affect.

Why

Why firmware and test

The work I have enjoyed most sits where software meets hardware: the moment a symptom on a screen turns into a signal I can measure.

Where problems hide

In RetroCore and on the LG lines, the hardest problems lived at the boundary between firmware and hardware: a pin that fails when the radio is on, a board that resets when the supply dips. Finding them took both the code and the scope.

Why it fits power tools

Battery-powered tools depend on firmware that decides how a pack charges, protects itself and talks to the tool. That firmware has to be tested against the spec, under real conditions, on every build. That is the kind of testing I want to do.

What I bring

Board bring-up and embedded C, signal-level debugging with an oscilloscope and logic analyzer, Python automation from a production line, and the habit of writing down what is confirmed and what is still open.

Happy to walk through any of this.
